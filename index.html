About

Listening for the pulse underneath the story.

Sound is the easiest path to a transcendent state. It can be completely immersive, and yet it is the most accessible of the arts. Close your eyes and you are in a world where meaning is made mostly from what you hear. It can be trance-like, meditative, introspective, or even frightening. Sound tells you what is inside you, even when you don't want to listen, or when you have been too busy to hear it.

I don't follow a single school of thought. My philosophy is still being built, out of art, everyday life, the cultures I have lived in, the people I meet and, above all, science. I studied physics before I studied sound, and the two keep meeting. A molecule, a forest, a city and a galaxy all swing between order and disorder, and sound, which is vibration and nothing else, is the most honest way I know to feel that.

I make work in a deeply personal way and then let it go. While a piece is being made, everything I am goes into it. Once it is finished, it belongs to whoever is listening, and I try not to hold on to what it should mean.

I want the same piece to reach everyone. Someone who has never thought about sound should simply be able to enjoy it. Someone who keeps coming back should find another layer each time, and then another. That is why, in my films, the sound leads and the image follows, and why I mix in Dolby Atmos for closeness rather than spectacle, so the listener sits inside a space instead of in front of it.

Trained at the Film and Television Institute of India, with a background in computer science, physics and mathematics.
